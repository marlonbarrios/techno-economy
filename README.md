
If territory is not only land or border but the place where resources, identities, and meanings are contested, **techno-economy** is one of its most intense dimensions, as it articulates capital and technology in a relationship that shapes territories, bodies, and modes of existence. There, the extractive logic of capital confronts practices of reciprocity and sustainability that sustain life.

Every technological advance responds to a model of accumulation, and at the same time, every economic logic drives technologies that reinforce that model. Thus, techno-economy produces not only productive systems but also forms of territoriality, power hierarchies, and subjectivities.

From industrial capitalism onward, economists like **Joseph Schumpeter** (mid-20th century) pointed out that technical innovation drives cycles of accumulation. Later, **Carlota Pérez** formulated the idea of **techno-economic paradigms**: great waves (steam, electricity, informatics) that reorganize technologies, capital, and society.  

Today, in the 21st century, **digitalization**, **biotechnology**, **artificial intelligence**, and **global platforms** are transforming not only production and consumption but also the control of physical and digital territories. **Platform economies** turn the network into a new space of territorial dispute, where **data and algorithms** function as the strategic resources of our time.  

Critical voices include **Manuel Castells** (*The Information Age*, 1996) and scholars of the political economy of the Internet like **Nick Srnicek** (*Platform Capitalism*, 2016).

---

## Decolonial Critical Perspective

From the perspective of **coloniality**, techno-economy is traversed by the same foundational power structures of otherness.  

- **Aníbal Quijano** showed how the *coloniality of power* articulates **race**, **labor**, and **technology** in a global system of exploitation.  
- **Walter Mignolo** and **Catherine Walsh** link these dynamics to the *coloniality of knowledge*: technologies reproduce **Eurocentric epistemologies** that legitimize territorial appropriation.  
- **Achille Mbembe** warns that **digital capitalism** articulates new forms of **surveillance** and **necropolitics**, where territory extends into the sphere of data and life itself.  

---

Territory, physical or digital, is not neutral : it is a field where **memory, power, and technology intersect**.  
Within this tension, **techno-economy** can be **imposition and dispossession**, or an **insurgent tool** to imagine other ways of inhabiting the world.

---

## A Pangea Perspective

From **Pangea**, to imagine a **decolonial techno-economy** is to think technologies and economies that do not reinforce the **extractivist logic of capital**, but **sustain life, reciprocity, and the pluriversal territory**:  
- community digital networks,  
- solidarity economies,  
- technological sovereignties, and  
- artificial intelligences situated in local contexts.  

Here, techno-economy is not just **technical analysis** but a **field of epistemic dispute**:

> What technologies are developed and in service of what economies?  
> Who controls the data, the algorithms, and the infrastructures that demarcate digital and material territories?  
> Which ways of life are legitimized and which expelled from the map?
